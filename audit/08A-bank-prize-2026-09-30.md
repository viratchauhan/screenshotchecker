## Defect and scope

The existing six-case agentic investigation test failed two cases: an account-suspension message directing verification at an IP link, and a prize asking for an upfront processing fee. Both fell through to a default CONSISTENT/no-action result. No real destination was opened.

The new narrow rules require explicit direct-action clauses rather than document-wide keyword co-occurrence. The bank rule requires an executed URL tool result whose URL appears in the text, a flagged/IP destination, account pressure, and an immediately preceding direct verification/unlock instruction. The prize rule requires a direct payment command whose fee is tied to the claimed prize. Negated and educational controls abstain. New evidence traces describe possible rule-based patterns and clearly state that identity, maliciousness or payment cannot be established from screenshot text.

Review iterations found false positives in negated/advice wording and unrelated shipping fees. Those examples are retained as regressions. Positive matching was tightened rather than treating a larger phrase blacklist as proof of intent. The final independent review accepted this bounded diff; this is not release/deployment approval.

## Limitations and remaining gates

This is deliberately limited English text-pattern coverage, not general natural-language understanding or an accuracy evaluation. Wording such as `Pay $50 processing fee to claim your prize now` may abstain, as can indirect instructions. Missing a rule is not evidence of safety. Original provider heuristics and its fallback CONSISTENT state remain unaudited; no broad claim that negation/education handling is solved throughout the product is justified.

No local browser/image OCR/network-privacy/mobile/keyboard acceptance was completed by this implementation batch.
